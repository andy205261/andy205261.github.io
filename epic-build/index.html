The gap

Where the signal gets lost

ICANS announces itself gradually. A patient scores 10 at the morning assessment, 9 at the evening one, 7 overnight. Each individual score looks survivable. The slope is what matters, and the slope is invisible when scores live as free text in a progress note and each shift sees only its own assessment.

The advisory

This is where most alerting projects go wrong. A fixed threshold — fire at ICE ≤ 7 — misses the patient who falls from 10 to 8 in six hours and fires repeatedly on the stable patient who sits at 7 for three days. The criterion is the drop, not the level.

BPA specificationInterruptive · care team
Trigger event
On filing of ICE — Total score.
Population
Inpatient · cell therapy or designated ICU bed · Cell therapy day between D+1 and D+30.
Criteria
Fires when either condition is met:
  • Current total is lower than the previous total by the configured drop or more, within a 24-hour lookback
  • Current total ≤ 6 (ICANS grade 2 or worse) and no advisory has fired for this patient in the suppression window
Suppression
One fire per patient per configured window, reset by an acknowledge reason of Provider notified or SmartSet opened.
Recipients
Filing nurse at the point of documentation; in-basket message to the covering provider on grade 3 or worse.
Display
Current score, previous score with its timestamp, the delta, the derived grade, and a 72-hour sparkline.
Acknowledge reasons
  • Provider notified — time stamped
  • ICANS SmartSet opened
  • Score explained by sedation or sleep — requires comment
  • Assessment error, re-documenting
Not built
No auto-ordering, no auto-paging of rapid response, no chart closure block. The advisory informs a clinician; it does not act.

Drop rates are modeled, not measured — the proportion of assessments showing a 2-point decline is an assumption until the rows exist and six weeks of real data can be queried. That retrospective query is the first post-go-live task, and the trigger is re-tuned from it.

Clarity validation queryRun weekly for the first six weeks
-- ICE score trajectory by encounter, cell therapy unit, last 60 days
-- Validates: rows filing, totals calculating, drops detectable
SELECT
    enc.PAT_ENC_CSN_ID,
    rec.RECORDED_TIME,
    MAX(CASE WHEN grp.FLO_MEAS_NAME = 'ICE TOTAL SCORE'
             THEN TRY_CAST(meas.MEAS_VALUE AS INT) END) AS ice_total,
    LAG(MAX(CASE WHEN grp.FLO_MEAS_NAME = 'ICE TOTAL SCORE'
                  THEN TRY_CAST(meas.MEAS_VALUE AS INT) END))
        OVER (PARTITION BY enc.PAT_ENC_CSN_ID
               ORDER BY rec.RECORDED_TIME) AS prior_total
FROM IP_FLWSHT_REC    rec
JOIN IP_FLWSHT_MEAS   meas ON meas.FSD_ID = rec.FSD_ID
JOIN IP_FLO_GP_DATA   grp  ON grp.FLO_MEAS_ID = meas.FLO_MEAS_ID
JOIN PAT_ENC_HSP      enc  ON enc.INPATIENT_DATA_ID = rec.INPATIENT_DATA_ID
WHERE grp.FLO_MEAS_NAME LIKE 'ICE %'
  AND rec.RECORDED_TIME >= DATEADD(day, -60, GETDATE())
GROUP BY enc.PAT_ENC_CSN_ID, rec.RECORDED_TIME
ORDER BY enc.PAT_ENC_CSN_ID, rec.RECORDED_TIME;
Three things this answers in week one: are all five component rows filing or only the total, are any encounters charting ICE outside the intended units, and what is the real distribution of between-assessment drops — the number the alert model above only assumes.

Governance

How it moves to production

Request

Intake and clinical ownership

Submitted by the cell therapy unit with a named physician owner. Without a clinician who owns the advisory's logic, nobody can approve a change to it later, and nobody is accountable for what it tells a nurse to do.

Design

Specification and governance review

This document, reviewed by clinical informatics and the alerting governance committee. The alert burden model is the part the committee actually cares about.

Build → Test

Non-production build and unit test

Rows and advisory built in the non-production environment, unit tested against the script above, then moved for integration testing with the SmartSet and in-basket routing.

Validate

Clinical validation

Two nurses and the physician owner chart a full simulated patient course end to end, including a false-positive case. Sign-off is a signature, not a verbal agreement.

Production

Release and monitoring

Released in a scheduled window with the tip sheet distributed 48 hours ahead. Rollback is a single advisory deactivation; the flowsheet rows stay, since charted data should not disappear. Fire counts and acknowledge reasons reviewed weekly for six weeks, then the trigger is re-tuned from real drop rates.

What this is, and is not

A specification exercise built on public clinical standards — the ASTCT consensus grading for ICANS — using invented workflow details and placeholder record IDs.

  • No patient data, no Stanford Health Care build content, no screenshots of a production system
  • Record IDs, unit names and protocol specifics are placeholders, filled at real build
  • Drop rates and alert volumes are modeled assumptions, labeled as such, to be replaced by the Clarity query above
  • Any real version of this requires clinical ownership, pharmacy review of the SmartSet, and approval through institutional alerting governance
