Module 2 · 3.2.1

AI Ethics in Healthcare

Ethics translates moral principles — patient safety, fairness, accountability — into design requirements. Regulation is ethics with teeth.

Framing

Scope

  • Ethics governs how data is used, how recommendations are made, how patient autonomy is preserved
  • Ethics + regulation together → trust & compliance built into development, not a late-stage burden

Motivating examples

  • Early oncology AI on limited datasets → bias → need for diverse validation
  • Early AI alert tools → alarm fatigue → stronger usability & performance testing standards

Ethical vs policy vectors

Ethical Vectors

  • Algorithmic Accountability — explainability & audit trails
  • Bias Mitigation — diverse datasets, guard against inequities
  • Patient Autonomy — protect consent, enable human review

Policy Vectors

  • FDA Safety Testing — safety & performance standards
  • HIPAA Privacy Safeguards — privacy & data governance
  • Global Frameworks — EU AI Act & UNESCO ethics charters

FDA — clinical AI gatekeeper

Purpose

  • Regulates SaMD (Software as a Medical Device) by risk & function
  • PCCP (Predetermined Change Control Plan) — predefined rules for post-approval model updates
  • IDx-DR — first FDA-cleared autonomous diagnostic AI (diabetic retinopathy screening)

Developer implications

  • Traceability: model inputs → clinical claims
  • Real-world validation + bias-monitoring pipelines
  • Update rules under PCCP; post-market surveillance & audit logs

HIPAA boundary conditions

The rules

  • Applies to PHI handled by covered entities
  • Minimum Necessary Rule — only essential data for the AI purpose
  • De-identification: Safe Harbor (remove 18 identifiers) or Expert Determination (low re-ID risk)
  • BAAs govern vendor privacy & breach responsibility

Developer implications

  • Train with synthetic or federated data, not real PHI
  • Data minimization + audit logs in pipelines
  • Sign BAAs with AI/cloud vendors handling PHI
  • Privacy-first design → faster approvals

IRB vs IDE

Research → IRB

  • AI projects using patient data / human subjects
  • Protects rights, privacy, consent
  • Reviews AI risks: bias, automation scope, accountability

Clinical → IDE

  • AI tested in live clinical settings
  • FDA oversight as a medical device
  • Safety & effectiveness validation; IRB + FDA protocols + continuous monitoring
Best practice

Use early decision trees with compliance/legal input to define when a system moves from research → clinical use.

Dataset Bias and Fairness

Bias = systematic under/misrepresentation of patient groups. It enters at every lifecycle stage, and "objective" data isn't immune.

Definitions + lifecycle

Dataset Bias

  • Ex 1: cardiac risk model trained on middle-aged white men → less reliable for women of color with different symptom patterns
  • Ex 2: sepsis algorithm on adult data → misses pediatric early-warning signs

Fairness

  • Equitable model performance across all subgroups
  • Equal quality of care, not just equal outcomes
  • Clinical factors — not demographics — guide recommendations

6 fairness metrics

Demographic Parity

Equal outcomes across population groups

Equalized Odds

Equal TPR & FPR across groups

Individual Fairness

Similar individuals → similar recommendations

Procedural Fairness

Transparent, consistent processes

Calibration

Predicted probabilities match actual outcomes per group

Counterfactual Fairness

Decision unchanged if sensitive attribute flips

6 bias types

Selection

Who's in the dataset ≠ who needs care: access barriers, site differences, exclusions

Representation

Unequal sample size: sparse data, unstable features, missing demographics

Measurement

Inaccurate capture: device errors, inconsistent units, subjective labels

Documentation

Language/template bias in notes: subjective wording, provider tone

Historical

Discrimination in legacy data: trial exclusions, outdated protocols

Confirmation

Clinical assumptions in labels: stereotype-driven diagnoses, selective documentation

Module 2 · 3.2.3

Responsible Deployment of AI Systems

Governance tiers, the 5-level transparency hierarchy, XAI techniques (LIME/SHAP), and multi-jurisdictional compliance.

Why responsible AI matters

Current situation

  • AI adoption accelerating across healthcare, finance, critical infrastructure
  • Public trust may decline from high-profile failures
  • Evolving global regulatory frameworks

Healthcare impact — 4

  • Patient Safety — life-or-death errors
  • Social Equity — biased models widen disparities
  • Legal Liability — rising accountability
  • Public Trust — essential for adoption
Path forward

Organizations implementing responsible AI frameworks gain competitive advantage through trust, compliance, innovation.

Institutional governance — 3 tiers

Tier 1 — Strategic Oversight

  • AI Ethics & Governance Board
  • Risk & Compliance Committee
  • Compliance Office

Tier 2 — Operational Mgmt

  • AI Center of Excellence (CoE)
  • Data Governance Council
  • Change Management Team

Tier 3 — Implementation

  • AI Development Teams
  • Clinical & Operational Users
  • QA Teams

Spanning all three: Continuous Learning, Improvement and Audit.

Explainability — definition + 5 reasons

AI Explainability

Ability to understand and articulate how an AI system arrives at decisions in terms humans can comprehend and act on.

  • Clinical Trust — physicians must understand before acting
  • Patient Safety — catch errors before harm
  • Regulatory Compliance — FDA, EU AI Act require it for high-risk devices
  • Accountability — clear attribution when things go wrong
  • Bias Detection — reveal hidden model biases

5-level transparency hierarchy

L1 Input
data used & prep
L2 Process
design & training
L3 Output
predictions + confidence
L4 Rationale
LIME, SHAP
L5 Impact
group performance

Layers: L1–L2 = Foundation, L3–L4 = Technical, L5 = Governance.

XAI techniques

Tier 1 — LIME

  • Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations
  • Simpler surrogate models approximate complex behavior; explains individual predictions locally
  • Ex: highlights pixels in medical images that drove diagnosis

Tier 1 — SHAP

  • SHapley Additive exPlanations — game theory: contribution of each feature
  • Ex: elevated BNP (+0.4) + normal BP (0.2) → heart failure risk score

Tier 2 — Causal & Concept-based

  • Counterfactuals: "temp 98.6°F instead of 102°F → sepsis risk 78% → 23%" (intervention effects)
  • Concept-based: "pneumonia: lung consolidation + air bronchograms + elevated WBC"

Tier 2 — Uncertainty-aware

  • Confidence intervals & reliability measures
  • Critical for edge cases & rare conditions

Multi-jurisdictional compliance

Healthcare-specific

  • FDA SaMD — device software
  • HIPAA — patient data privacy
  • CLIA — lab standards for AI diagnostics

General AI

  • EU AI Act — risk-based rules
  • GDPR — data protection
  • State laws — CA, IL & others

Industry standards

  • ISO/IEC 23053 — AI risk mgmt
  • IEEE — ethical design & transparency
  • NIST — voluntary AI risk framework

Implementation — 4 phases

1 Foundation
governance, policies, readiness, training
2 Pilot
low-risk high-value; monitoring; feedback
3 Scale & Optimize
expand pilots, refine, alerts
4 Maturity
culture, safeguards, ethical innovation

Measuring — 3 metric families

Governance

Policy compliance %, risk assessment coverage, stakeholder satisfaction

Technical

Accuracy/precision/recall, explainability quality, uptime/reliability

Ethical

Bias detection across groups, privacy compliance, transparency scores

Emerging trends

Challenges

  • Federated Learning — train across institutions without data sharing
  • Edge AI — local devices, real-time
  • Multimodal AI — text + images + more

Solutions

  • Differential Privacy — math for privacy-preserving analysis
  • Federated Analytics — insights without centralizing
  • XAI Standards

Path forward

  • Continuous learning
  • Collaborative innovation with regulators
  • Ethical leadership

Self-check — tap to reveal

Agent loop, in order — and the 3 coordination mechanisms?
Perceive → Decide → Act → Interact. Coordination: Negotiation (priorities/resources), Task Allocation (dynamic), Consensus (shared belief).
Expand FIPA's four components.
ACL — messages INFORM/REQUEST/AGREE/REFUSE. AMS — identities & permissions. DF — service discovery. AP — runtime environment.
CareScore AI: university, 3 modules, impact number?
Rosalind Franklin University. Case Management / Synthesis / Scoring. Impact: >70% reduction in manual review (baseline 30–45 min/student, >72 hr delays).
What makes MedAgentSim self-evolving?
Outcomes write back to Medical & Experience Record Buffers → kNN few-shot pulls better examples over time. Diagnosis = majority-vote ensembling over chain-of-thought doctor agents.
Four interoperability standards and what each covers?
FHIR — exchange API. SNOMED-CT — clinical terminology. ICD-10 — diagnoses. CPT — procedures/billing.
What is IDx-DR and why does it show up twice in Module 2?
First FDA-cleared autonomous diagnostic AI — diabetic retinopathy screening. Appears again as the compliance-value example: FDA authorization + CE marking (Class IIa) = dual compliance streamlined EU deployment.
What is a PCCP and which agency framework pairs with it?
Predetermined Change Control Plan — predefined rules for post-approval model updates & validation of adaptive algorithms. Pairs with FDA's SaMD regulation and TPLC (total product life cycle: pre + post-market) review model.
HIPAA's two de-identification standards?
Safe Harbor — remove 18 identifiers. Expert Determination — documented low re-identification risk. Bonus pairs: Minimum Necessary Rule; BAAs for vendors handling PHI.
IRB vs IDE — which is which?
IRB (Institutional Review Board) = research use — patient data/human subjects, protects rights/privacy/consent. IDE (Investigational Device Exemption) = clinical use — live settings, FDA oversight as a medical device, IRB + FDA protocols + continuous monitoring.
How does the EU AI Act classify healthcare AI, and how does WHO guidance differ?
EU AI Act: risk-tiered, legally binding — healthcare AI is "high-risk". WHO (2021 & 2023): non-binding, focuses on equity/autonomy/accountability, influences procurement & ethics policies.
Name all 6 fairness metrics and distinguish Equalized Odds from Calibration.
Demographic Parity, Equalized Odds, Individual, Procedural, Calibration, Counterfactual. Equalized Odds = equal TPR & FPR across groups. Calibration = predicted probabilities match actual outcomes within each group.
Name the 6 dataset bias types and the slide's two classic examples.
Selection, Representation, Measurement, Documentation, Historical, Confirmation. Examples: cardiac risk model trained on middle-aged white men → unreliable for women of color; sepsis algorithm on adults → misses pediatric warning signs.
Walk the 5-level transparency hierarchy and its 3 layers.
L1 Input → L2 Process (Foundation layer) → L3 Output → L4 Rationale — LIME/SHAP (Technical layer) → L5 Impact (Governance layer).
LIME vs SHAP — mechanism and example for each.
LIME: builds simpler local surrogate models to explain individual predictions — e.g., highlights image pixels driving a diagnosis. SHAP: game theory — each feature's contribution — e.g., elevated BNP (+0.4) + normal BP (0.2) in a heart-failure risk score.
Give the counterfactual explainability example with numbers.
"If temperature was 98.6°F instead of 102°F, sepsis risk drops from 78% to 23%." Used to understand intervention effects.
Sort into buckets: CLIA, GDPR, NIST, FDA SaMD, EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 23053.
Healthcare-specific: FDA SaMD, CLIA (+ HIPAA). General AI regulation: EU AI Act, GDPR (+ state laws). Industry standards: ISO/IEC 23053, NIST (+ IEEE).
Three governance tiers and one body in each?
Tier 1 Strategic Oversight — AI Ethics & Governance Board. Tier 2 Operational Management — AI Center of Excellence. Tier 3 Implementation & Delivery — AI Development Teams. All wrapped in continuous learning & audit.
Four implementation phases in order?
Foundation (governance, policies, readiness) → Pilot (low-risk high-value, monitoring) → Scale & Optimize → Maturity & Innovation (governance embedded in culture).
Federated learning vs differential privacy — which solves what?
Federated learning: train models across institutions without sharing raw data (a next-gen challenge). Differential privacy: mathematical techniques for privacy-preserving analysis (an emerging solution).
Mayo Clinic model: 4 practices + 4 quadrants?
Practices: multidisciplinary steering committee; mandatory pre-deployment validation & bias assessment; continuous monitoring dashboards; standardized AI incident response. Quadrants: Ethical Evaluation & Usage · Legal/Regulatory/Compliance · Clinical & Scientific Verification · Org Deployment & Change Management.
